#### 31.19 THE MEASUREMENT OF EVERY MODEL: speed, trust, long prompts and hidden limits — 2026-11-16

*Done at the user's request, before any routing design: "the speed and the trust of all models still need to be explored". It measures SPEED and TRUST only, never how well a model does a job. Exit `88.99.140.30`, AS24940 Hetzner, Falkenstein; the Google probe answered 200 first. Time: 14:39 to 15:41 local. **Everything is in ONE file: `artifacts/slice11-measure/results.jsonl`** (git-ignored, 973 lines: 452 requests, 380 calls, 71 model summaries and 70 probe requests, with the headers of every answer). The tools are `measure_models.py`, `repeats.py`, `probes.py`, `analyze.py` and `final_tables.py` (copied to `artifacts/slice11-measure/tools/`, together with the changed files of the spreader copy). Nothing in `labpilot/` was changed.*

**The rules of the test (the user's), and where they were bent.**

1. **71 models, five calls each.** Every live and NOT_TRUSTED tier of `CHAIN` (64 different models: a Google model on three keys counts once), plus Ministral 3B (not in the chain) and six Cohere chat models. The 36 tiers of `KNOWN_DEAD` were not called again. The five calls are five prompt sizes: **100, 200, 500, about 4,000 tokens, and one LONG prompt.** The long prompt is 20,000 tokens, except where the model, the provider or the quota cannot take it: Gemma on Google 13,000 (an input room of 16,000 tokens a minute), Groq 6,000 (8,000 tokens a minute counting prompt and answer), Cloudflare 8,000 (neurons), Bazaarlink 8,000 (60 units a day), Cohere tiny-aya 6,000 (8,192 window). The real size is the one the provider reports: 19,260 to 19,810 tokens for most 20,000 targets (Cohere 17,567 to 19,370, MiniMax 18,616 to 18,656).
2. **No timeout, no output limit, no input limit of ours.** No `max_tokens` is sent and none of our length checks runs, so the answer ends where the model ends. Only a stop at 1,200 s per read exists, for a call that never ends. **That stop failed once:** one Token Harbor call (MiMo V2.6 Flash, 200 tokens) was still open after 40 minutes, because the server kept the connection alive and a read timeout only fires when no byte arrives. I stopped the run by hand, logged the call as failed, and ran the rest of Token Harbor, the second sitting and the probes with a wall-clock stop (600 s and 300 s).
3. **Two retries, after 5 s and 10 s.** A call is VALID when the answer is HTTP 200, not empty, finished by itself (not cut), and has no `<think>` text in it. Two retries are not used when the provider says the daily quota or the capacity is spent (a `Retry-After` over 120 s); that was set by a rule that also caught the words "all fallbacks were exhausted", so two Requesty calls went without retries and were repeated in the second sitting.
4. **TRUST = at least 4 of the 5 calls valid** (after the retries). The log also keeps how many were valid on the FIRST try (column "first try"): if that stricter reading is used, **62 models** are trusted, and the two that fall out are Gemini 3.8 Flash (3 clean) and Muse Glimmer on Requesty (2 clean). **Two failed calls end the test of a model**, because 4 of 5 can no longer be reached.
5. **Every prompt is real code** (our own `labpilot/` files, cut at a line end) with two random codes, one at the start and one at the end, and the same instruction: "reply with one line: A=..., B=..., one sentence about the code". The codes also defeat caches (Routeway keeps identical requests for an hour). Whether the model quoted both codes is a SEPARATE mark (`n` in the table), not part of trust: it shows a model that did not read the whole prompt (weak model or silent cut). No prompt was cut silently: the reported prompt tokens are linear in the size for every model.
6. **Pacing with the spreader copy.** Rooms (tokens and requests a minute) and slots from the scratch copy `s11` paced the calls, and the script also read the limit headers of every answer to wait before the next call (a per-minute 429 cannot be cured by a retry after 5 s). Lanes (one for each provider account, and one for each Google model) ran at the same time; inside a lane the calls ran one by one.
7. **A second sitting** repeated the suspect calls once (a number far from the model's other numbers, or a failure): 32 calls, marked in the last column. **A third part** (the probes) sent 2 to 5 tiny calls to each provider to find limits that nobody states: several at the same moment, back to back, and with a pause.

**What the table says (the speed).**

- **The length of the prompt barely matters.** The median of all valid calls: 100 tokens 3.8 s, 200 tokens 3.7 s, 500 tokens 3.3 s, 4,000 tokens 4.4 s, 20,000 tokens 5.0 s. A 20,000-token prompt costs about one second more than a 100-token one. What costs time is the answer: a model that reasons for 1,000 tokens needs seconds more than one that answers in 40.

- **Tokens:** the real number of characters for one token was 3.58 to 4.18 (median 3.87). Our estimate `chars / 3` counts about 29% too many tokens. That is the safe direction, and it means a "20,000-token" prompt by our estimate is only about 15,500 real tokens.

**How to read tokens and tokens per second in Table 1, and what they do NOT say.**
- **The seconds are the whole wait** (queue, reading the prompt, writing). So tokens per second here is a LOWER bound of the real writing speed.
- **Most answers were short.** The test asked for one line, so 35 to 60 tokens is normal (Mistral, Cohere small models, the Routeway Gemma finetunes, LiteRouter Qwen). For a 40-token answer the time is mostly the wait before the first word, so a low number there (6 to 25) shows the wait and NOT a slow writer.
- **Models that think write many tokens for the same question** (Nemotron 3.5 Lightning about 1,000, Hy4 preview about 970, Gemini Flash-Lite about 365, Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite about 437). They were not "longer answers": most of it is thinking.
- **The only rows that show real writing speed are the ones with 200 or more tokens written:** Groq 95 to 205, Cohere command-a-plus 105, North Mini Code 115 to 125, LiteRouter DeepSeek and MiniMax 50 to 75, Token Harbor DeepSeek 33 to 51, Nemotron 3 Ultra 39 to 41, Hy4 35, Requesty Gemma 4 31B 29, Google Gemma 4 31B 15 (it spends its time waiting, 25 s), Requesty Muse Glimmer 4, Gemini 3.8 Flash about 1.
- **A long report was NOT measured.** The longest answers here are about 1,000 tokens (and one runaway of 63,034 from Cohere north-mini-code, 200 s). A report of 3,000 tokens would take about 15 to 30 s on a model at 100 to 200 tokens per second and about 85 s at 35. To know the real writing speed we need one call per candidate model that asks for a long answer (about 1,500 tokens). This is open.

**Strength: the rating 1 to 5 of every model (added 2026-11-16, read from Artificial Analysis and other sources the same day).** The rating is the third thing a task needs beside quota and speed. It says how clever the model is, NOT how well it did a job in our tests.

*The scale.* The newest Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index is **v5.0.1** (v5 came on 2026-10-27, with 60% private test sets). It is much harder than the v4.x we used before: the leader scores 58, the best free models only about 29 to 35, and models that scored 23 to 24 on v4.3.2 now score 4 to 12. So the strength bands are on the v5.0.1 scale:

| strength | v5.0.1 score | what it is good for |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 29 or more | the best free models: the strong reasoning jobs (audit, skeptic, method, concept, the two-file report) |
| 4 | 20 to 28 | strong enough for most jobs, as a second choice for the hardest ones |
| 3 | 10 to 19 | middle jobs (planner, claims, trace, judge, merge, one-file answer) |
| 2 | 5 to 9 | easy jobs (gate, rewrite, short summary, chat question) |
| 1 | under 5 | only the easiest jobs (a gate, a yes or no, a short rewrite) |

*Where a model has no v5.0.1 score* its older score was moved to the v5 scale with the models that have both: v4.3.2 minus about 11 to 14 (Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite 23 → 12, Gemma 4 31B 15 → 4, Qwen3.8 27B 34 → 20, GLM-5.3 Flash 42 → 28, Gemini 3.8 Flash 41 → 31). A model with no Artificial Analysis score at all was rated from another benchmark or from its family, and its confidence is low. The confidence of every rating is in the last column of Table 1 and in the log. **Scores from different index versions are never put in one comparison.**

*What changed against the old order of the chain.* The chain was ordered with the old v4.3.2 scores. On the new scale: GLM-5.3 Flash (our old tier 1, 42) is 28, so strength 4, not 5; Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite is 12 (3) and Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite 6 (2); **GPT-OSS 120B and Gemma 4 31B are 4 (strength 1)**; Nemotron 3 Ultra 12 (3); North Mini Code 3 (1); Command A Plus 6 (2). The registry order was NOT changed by this work; the next routing table should use these ratings.

*Sources.* The Artificial Analysis leaderboard and open-source page (v5.0.1, read 2026-11-16, the main source), the v5 article of 2026-10-27, the model pages and launch articles of Artificial Analysis for the older scores, Epoch AI and aggregator sites (benchlm, llmbase, datalearner, Routeway) only where Artificial Analysis had nothing. Research notes of the three searches: AA v5.0.1 has NO score for Gemini 3.6 Flash, Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite (6 only on its deprecated page), Gemma 4 26B, DeepSeek V3, V4 Flash, GPT-OSS 20B, MiniMax M2.7, Qwen3 Coder Next, Qwen3 Next 80B, MiMo V2.5, Hy3, Codestral, Ministral, Mistral Nemo, Cohere Command A, R and R7B, Tiny Aya (shown as N/A or "--"); the rating of those is an estimate.

*Limits.* (1) The rating is a ranking of general cleverness, not of our jobs: a model with 1 can still be the right choice for a yes-or-no gate, and a model with 5 can be too slow or too small a quota for it. (2) The Gemma finetunes on Routeway have no score of their own; they got the stock model's rating. (3) Two matches rest on the name only (Muse Glimmer 30B = "Muse Glimmer High" on the page; Hy3 = Hy3 preview). (4) Versions that Artificial Analysis does not state are marked low. (5) The scores change when a new index version comes: re-read them before the routing table is final.
